# 🗣 Child Boundary, Exit & Help Scripts
*Empowering Children with Practical, Non-Escalatory Scripts*

When children face teasing, exclusion, or verbal targeting, they often freeze because their brains go into a "fight, flight, or freeze" panic. They do not have words ready. 

Giving children short, practiced, repeatable phrases builds muscle memory. This guide details the three core response types: **Boundary Scripts**, **Exit Scripts**, and **Help-Seeking Scripts**.

---

## 🛑 1. Boundary Scripts: "Stop. I don't like that."
*A boundary script is designed to establish a clear, firm, non-defensive wall. It does not trade insults or argue—it simply states truth.*

> [!TIP]
> **Rules for Boundaries:**
> * Keep your back straight and look the other person in the eye.
> * Speak in a strong, normal talking voice (do not shout or whisper).
> * Keep your hands relaxed by your sides.

### Word-for-Word Boundary Scripts:
* *"Stop. I don't like that."*
* *"That is not okay. Stop doing that."*
* *"Please don't speak to me that way."*
* *"We are not doing this. Stop."*

---

## 🏃 2. Exit Scripts: "I'm walking away."
*An exit script gives the child a verbal bridge to leave the situation immediately. It ensures they exit on their own terms without arguing or trying to win a fight.*

> [!TIP]
> **Rules for Exits:**
> * Say your line clearly, turn immediately, and walk toward a safe, populated area.
> * Do not wait around to see how the other person reacts.
> * Do not look back.

### Word-for-Word Exit Scripts:
* *"I'm walking away now."*
* *"I have somewhere else to be. Bye."*
* *"I'm not going to stand here and listen to this."*
* *"I'm going to go join the other group."*

---

## 🙋 3. Help-Seeking Scripts: "I need a trusted adult."
*Many children hesitate to seek help because they don't want to be called a "tattletale." Teach your child the difference between tattling (trying to get someone in trouble for a minor infraction) and reporting (protecting safety and boundaries).*

### Word-for-Word Reporting Scripts:
* *"I need help. [Name] is repeatedly crossing my boundaries, and I've asked them to stop."*
* *"I am feeling unsafe. I need you to step in."*
* *"Can you please help me? I'm trying to walk away and [Name] is blocking me."*
* *"I am reporting this because it's not safe."*

---

## 🎭 Parent-Child Practice Roleplay Guide
*Practice these scripts at home without pressure. Keep it fun and light so your child builds confidence rather than anxiety.*

### Exercise 1: The "Mirror Mirror" (Posture Check)
1. Stand in front of your child. 
2. Ask them to practice standing with "courage posture": shoulders back, head up, hands relaxed, feet slightly apart.
3. Have them say their boundary script (*"Stop. I don't like that."*) while holding that posture for 5 seconds. Praise their strong posture!

### Exercise 2: The "Say and Go" (Exit Practice)
1. You act as the mild instigator (e.g., saying something slightly silly or teasing in a playful way, like *"Hey, you can't play here"*).
2. Have your child say their exit line (*"I'm walking away now."*).
3. Have them immediately turn and walk to the other side of the room.
4. High-five them and celebrate their successful exit!

### Exercise 3: The "Tattling vs. Reporting" Game
Read these scenarios to your child and ask them: *"Is this tattling, or is this reporting?"*

* **Scenario A**: *"Joey took the blue crayon when I wanted to use it."* 
  * *Answer*: **Tattling** (You can handle this by asking or using another color).
* **Scenario B**: *"Sarah keeps calling me mean names on the bus every single day, and it makes my stomach hurt."*
  * *Answer*: **Reporting** (This is a repeated pattern of harm; you must tell an adult).
* **Scenario C**: *"Tommy fell off the swings and hit his knee, and he is crying."*
  * *Answer*: **Reporting** (Someone is hurt; they need immediate adult help).
